Flight Deck Manager and this website

Privacy Policy

Effective November 10, 2026

✓
Data Not CollectedThe Developer does not collect, receive or store any personal information from this app.

This Privacy Policy describes how Flight Deck Manager and the Flight Deck Suite website (the “Software”), developed by Malcolm Syme (the “Developer”), handles information. The Software operates entirely on your own computer.

1. Information We Collect

The Developer does not collect any personal information. The Software and the website have no user accounts, advertising, analytics, cookies or tracking, and nothing is sent to the Developer.

2. The Website

The website is hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any website, GitHub receives your device’s IP address and the pages you open, under GitHub’s own privacy statement. Downloads are served from GitHub’s release storage in the same way. The website stores nothing in your browser.

3. Information Stored on Your Computer

The Software stores the following information locally, on your computer only:

Application data — AppData\Local\Flight Deck Suite\ManagerSettings, logs, the list of installed apps and their versions, downloads not yet installed, and copies of the simulator’s exe.xml file saved before it is changed.
The programs — AppData\Local\Programs\Flight Deck SuiteThe Manager and the apps you install, each with a Start menu entry.

To check what an app needs, the Software looks on your computer for Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024, vPilot, Microsoft Edge WebView2, Tailscale, OBS Studio and FSLTL, by reading where they are installed and the simulator’s own settings file, reads OBS Studio’s own settings to see whether its WebSocket server is switched on, and asks Tailscale’s own program on your computer whether it is signed in. These checks take place on your computer and nothing is transmitted.

“Copy details for support” copies a summary of these checks, the apps’ versions and the latest lines of the Manager’s log to your clipboard, with your Windows user name and your computer’s name left out. Nothing is sent unless you paste it into a message yourself.

4. Third-Party Services

The Software contacts the following services only to provide the features described:

  • GitHub (github.com, api.github.com and GitHub’s release storage): to check for new versions of the Manager and of the apps, at most about every 12 hours and when you choose Check now, and to download the versions you install. No information about you is sent.
  • The Windows Package Manager (winget) and the programs’ official sources: only when you choose “Install for me” for OBS Studio, Tailscale or Microsoft Edge WebView2, Windows’ own installer downloads that program from Microsoft’s winget catalogue and the program’s official download site.

Each of these services may receive your device’s IP address, as with any internet request, and is governed by its own terms and privacy policy. The Developer does not control these services and is not responsible for their practices.

5. Children’s Privacy

The Software does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

6. Your Choices

You may turn off automatic update checks in Settings, remove any app (choosing whether to keep its data), or delete all stored information by removing the folders listed in Section 3 and uninstalling the Software.

7. Changes to This Policy

If the way the Software handles information changes, this Privacy Policy will be updated and the effective date at the top of this page will change.

8. Contact

Logbook, with Playback and Hangar

Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

✓
Data Not CollectedThe Developer does not collect, receive or store any personal information from this app.

This Privacy Policy describes how Logbook (the “Software”), developed by Malcolm Syme (the “Developer”), handles information. The Software operates entirely on your own computer.

1. Information We Collect

The Developer does not collect any personal information. The Software has no user accounts, advertising, analytics or tracking, and it does not transmit your logbook to the Developer or to any third party.

2. Information Stored on Your Computer

The Software stores the following information locally, on your computer only:

Logbook — Documents\Flight Deck Suite\LogbookFlight records (times, route, landing details, approach recording, flight plan, arrival weather report and, with Ramp running, the stands you left from and parked at), the logbook spreadsheet, this logbook page, flight-hour totals and daily backups.
Application data — AppData\Local\Flight Deck Suite\LogbookSettings, airport and runway data, logs, Playback’s list of your recordings and clips, the latest list of VATSIM events, Hangar’s list of your add-ons from its last scan, a live status file that Squawk reads on the same computer, and downloaded VATSIM airport data. If you use Share my flight: its settings, the share page and snapshot last uploaded, the copies of the landing videos you share and, if you set up the always-on page, the name of its GitHub repository and your key for it, encrypted for your Windows account.
Windows sign-inOnly if you turn on Start with Windows: one entry in your Windows account’s list of programs to start when you sign in. Turning it off removes it.
Playback — Flight Deck Suite\Playback in Documents, Videos and PicturesRecordings of your landings (and takeoffs, if turned on), aircraft profiles, and the video clips and screenshots you choose to take. While a clip records, the name of your OBS profile and scene is noted so OBS can be put back as it was.
This browserDisplay preferences for this page, such as flights you have hidden.

To mark flights flown on VATSIM, the Software checks whether a VATSIM pilot client is running on your computer. To retrieve your flight plan, it uses the SimBrief username saved in its settings or by Squawk, if any. If Ramp is running, the Software reads Ramp’s status file on your computer for the stand you are on when a flight starts and ends. If you use Share my flight, the Software asks Squawk on your computer for the callsign and VATSIM ID you are connected with. These checks take place on your computer and nothing is transmitted.

Hangar reads the names, makers, versions, sizes and folder locations of the add-ons in your Community folder, and finds that folder in Microsoft Flight Simulator’s own settings file. It only reads them, on your computer. The list is shown only on this page on your computer and is never sent to the Developer or to any third party.

Settings can be changed from this logbook page on your computer. Opened any other way, this page is view only.

3. Third-Party Services

The Software contacts the following services only to provide the features described:

  • SimBrief (simbrief.com): if you have provided a SimBrief username, it is sent to retrieve your latest flight plan.
  • VATSIM weather service (metar.vatsim.net): the code of your arrival airport is sent to retrieve the weather report at the time of landing.
  • GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com): public airport and runway data from the OurAirports project is downloaded. No information about you is sent.
  • VATSIM events (my.vatsim.net): the public list of VATSIM events is downloaded when you open Home or Events, at most about every 30 minutes, and kept on your computer. No information about you is sent. When you open Events, your browser loads each event’s banner picture from the address VATSIM lists for it, without telling that server which page it is on.
  • Google Calendar (calendar.google.com): only if you choose Add to Google Calendar, your browser opens Google Calendar with that event’s name, times, airports and link filled in.
  • VATSIM data feed (data.vatsim.net): only while Share my flight is on, the public list of pilots and flight plans on the network is downloaded about every 30 seconds to find your own flight plan. No information about you is sent.
  • GitHub (VATSIM Spy data project, raw.githubusercontent.com): only while Share my flight is on, public VATSIM airport data is downloaded about once a week. No information about you is sent.
  • Tailscale: only while Share my flight is on, your share page and live flight are published through Tailscale Funnel, under Tailscale’s own privacy policy.
  • GitHub (api.github.com and GitHub Pages): only if you set up the always-on page, the share page, its snapshot and the landing videos you share (described in Section 5) are uploaded with your key to the public repository you chose, and GitHub stores and publishes them under its own terms and privacy policy.

Each of these services may receive your device’s IP address, as with any internet request, and is governed by its own terms and privacy policy. The Developer does not control these services and is not responsible for their practices.

To record video clips and take screenshots, the Software controls OBS Studio on your computer through its local connection. To connect, it reads OBS’s own connection settings on your computer (it never changes them). Before a replay, it asks Squawk on your computer whether you are connected to VATSIM. Neither of these sends anything over the internet.

4. Data Security

Because your information is stored on your own computer, its security depends on the security of that computer. Keeping your operating system up to date and maintaining backups is recommended.

5. Sharing Your Flight

Share my flight is off unless you turn it on in Settings. When it is on, the Software publishes a view-only page at your share link through Tailscale Funnel. The page shows only:

  • your callsign, aircraft type, and departure and arrival airports;
  • your live position, altitude, speed, heading, flight phase, estimated arrival time and distance to go;
  • your most recent landing (its rate, grade, crab angle at touchdown, airport, runway and time), during a flight as well as after it, and a summary of your most recent logged flight;
  • the landing videos you choose to share, each with its airport, runway, route, aircraft, landing rate, grade and date;
  • only if you turn on Show my career: your total time in the sim and in the air, distance flown, numbers of flights, airports, countries, aircraft types and flights on VATSIM, your best landing for each aircraft class and your recent landings, and, for each part you leave on, your last 30 days, your landing statistics, a map of the routes you have flown, your most flown aircraft and airports, your records and your milestones (landings with their date, route, aircraft, airport, runway, rate and grade), counting every flight in your logbook. A part of your career that you turn off is not sent to the page at all.

It never shows your name, your settings or any other information. The Software does not collect information about people who view the page; Tailscale delivers the page under its own privacy policy. When you turn Share my flight off, the page no longer shows your flight.

Landing videos. A landing video is shared only when you choose Share this landing on one of your clips. The Software makes a smaller copy of the clip (with the simulator’s sound only) for the page; the clip itself stays in your logbook. The page keeps your 20 most recently shared landings; older ones, and any you take down, are removed from the page but stay in your logbook.

The always-on page. If you set it up, a copy of the share page is kept on GitHub Pages so that it can be opened while your computer is off. After each logged flight, when you share or take down a landing video, and when you change these settings, the Software uploads a snapshot of the information above, other than your live position, and the shared landing videos to the public GitHub repository you chose, together with the address of your share link so that the copy can show your live flight while you fly. Each upload replaces everything before it, so the repository keeps no history of earlier snapshots or of videos you have taken down. Anyone can see the repository while it exists. When you turn Share my flight off, or remove the always-on page in Settings, the snapshot is replaced by a notice that you are not sharing; deleting the repository on GitHub removes everything.

6. Children’s Privacy

The Software does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

7. Your Choices

You may delete individual flights, Playback recordings and clips from this logbook page, turn off Playback’s automatic recording in its settings, turn off Share my flight, Show my career, any part of your career or landing videos in Settings, take down any shared landing video, remove the always-on page in Settings (and delete its repository on GitHub), remove your SimBrief username from Squawk’s settings or Logbook’s settings file, or delete all stored information by removing the folders listed in Section 2 and uninstalling the Software.

8. Changes to This Policy

If the way the Software handles information changes, this Privacy Policy will be updated and the effective date at the top of this page will change.

Squawk

Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

✓
Data Not CollectedThe Developer does not collect, receive or store any personal information from this app.

This Privacy Policy describes how Squawk (the “Software”), developed by Malcolm Syme (the “Developer”), handles information. The Software operates entirely on your own computer and iPhone.

1. Information We Collect

The Developer does not collect any personal information. The Software has no user accounts, advertising, analytics or tracking, and your information is never routed through a server operated by the Developer.

2. Information Stored on Your Devices

On your computer — vPilot’s Plugins folderSettings, quick replies, notification subscriptions for your devices, a record of recent notifications (their titles and delivery status), the key used to sign notifications, downloaded airspace data, the event reminders you have set (which events, and when to remind you) and diagnostic logs. Share my flight settings and files left by versions before 6.5 are kept there unchanged; Logbook reads them once when it takes Share my flight over.
In your computer’s memoryMessages, frequency text and flight details, held only while vPilot is running and discarded when it closes.
On your iPhoneApp settings and display preferences, your last callsign, which messages you have read, your PIN if you set one, keyboard measurements used for layout, and a copy of your latest flight, messages and logbook so the app can open without a connection.

3. How Information Moves Between Your Devices

Your iPhone and your computer communicate over your private Tailscale network, which encrypts the connection end to end. The Software also reads Logbook’s status and logbook files on the same computer and shows them on your iPhone over the same private connection; they are not sent anywhere else. Whether your aircraft is held after a replay, and your request to release it, are passed between Logbook and your iPhone the same way. While you are connected, the Software tells Logbook on the same computer your callsign and VATSIM ID, which Logbook uses for Share my flight if you turn it on; this answer is given only to programs on your computer and is not sent anywhere else.

4. Third-Party Services

The Software contacts the following services only to provide the features described:

  • Apple Push Notification service: notifications are encrypted on your computer before being passed to Apple for delivery to your iPhone. Apple cannot read their contents or who they are from.
  • VATSIM (data.vatsim.net, metar.vatsim.net, my.vatsim.net): public network data, weather reports and event listings are downloaded. Airport codes are sent for weather requests; no information about you is sent.
  • GitHub (VATSIM Spy data project): public airspace boundary data is downloaded.
  • Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com): the location of your destination airport is sent to determine its local time zone.
  • SimBrief (simbrief.com): if you have provided a SimBrief username, it is sent to retrieve your latest flight plan.
  • Tailscale: provides the private network connecting your devices, under Tailscale’s own privacy policy.

Each of these services may receive your device’s IP address, as with any internet request, and is governed by its own terms and privacy policy. The Developer does not control these services and is not responsible for their practices.

5. Data Security

Connections between your devices are encrypted, and you may protect the app with a PIN. Because information is stored on your own devices, its security also depends on the security of those devices and your Tailscale account.

6. Children’s Privacy

The Software does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

7. Your Choices

You may turn off any notification in Settings, remove your SimBrief username in Settings, delete the Software’s files from vPilot’s Plugins folder to erase the information stored on your computer, and remove the app from your Home Screen to erase the information stored on your iPhone.

8. Changes to This Policy

If the way the Software handles information changes, this Privacy Policy will be updated and the effective date at the top of this page will change.

Ramp

Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

✓
Data Not CollectedThe Developer does not collect, receive or store any personal information from this app.

This Privacy Policy describes how Ramp (the “Software”), developed by Malcolm Syme (the “Developer”), handles information. The Software operates entirely on your own computer.

1. Information We Collect

The Developer does not collect any personal information. The Software has no user accounts, advertising, analytics or tracking, and it does not send anything to the Developer. The only third-party services it contacts are described in Section 4.

2. Information Stored on Your Computer

The Software stores the following information locally, on your computer only:

Application data — AppData\Local\Flight Deck Suite\RampSettings, the stands you have reported, logs, the window’s own web view data (its size and position), copies of the simulator’s exe.xml file saved before Ramp changes it, a list of the aircraft Ramp has placed in the simulator (so that they can be removed after an unexpected stop), how many aircraft each airport could hold above your frame-rate setting, and the stand you are parked at (for Logbook, which shows it with your flight).
The program — AppData\Local\Programs\Flight Deck Suite\RampRamp itself and its airline data, with a Start menu entry.

To find your aircraft and liveries, the Software reads Microsoft Flight Simulator’s settings file and the names of the folders in your Community folder, and reads FSLTL’s rules file. While a flight runs, it reads your aircraft’s position and the aircraft around it from the simulator, and Logbook’s status file (to pause while a replay plays). If GSX’s Remote Control Server is switched on in GSX’s settings, it reads the gate you pick in GSX from GSX, on your computer. It only reads them, on your computer, and never sends them anywhere.

3. The Window

Ramp’s window is a page served by Ramp on your computer only; nothing else on your network can reach it. It is shown in Ramp’s own window with Microsoft Edge WebView2, which comes with Windows (or, if WebView2 isn’t available, in a Microsoft Edge window), using its own data folder in Ramp’s folder, with syncing turned off; WebView2 and Edge are provided by Microsoft under their own terms and privacy policy. The page itself loads nothing from the internet.

4. Third-Party Services

SimBrief (simbrief.com): to prepare your arrival airport, the Software reads the SimBrief username saved in Logbook’s or Squawk’s settings, if any, and sends it to SimBrief to retrieve your latest flight plan; only the origin and destination airports and the time the plan was made are used. Nothing else is sent. You can turn this off with “Prepare my arrival airport” in Settings.

VATSIM (data.vatsim.net): while Ramp is filling an airport and “Make room for VATSIM pilots” is on, the Software downloads VATSIM’s public network data about every 30 seconds, to see which stands other pilots are parked on and to show their callsigns in the window. Nothing about you is sent. Only each pilot’s callsign, position, altitude, ground speed and heading, and the aircraft type in their flight plan (for its wingspan), are used, kept in memory only; Ramp recognises your own entry by comparing it with your aircraft’s position, so that you are never counted as another pilot. Ramp’s log may name the callsigns it made way for and where those aircraft were; it doesn’t record your own callsign. Turning “Make room for VATSIM pilots” off stops these downloads.

5. Children’s Privacy

The Software does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

6. Your Choices

You may change any setting in the window, remove your reports by deleting the reports folder, or delete all stored information by removing the folders listed in Section 2 and uninstalling the Software (Ramp’s uninstaller removes the program and its Start menu entry, and offers to remove its data).

7. Changes to This Policy

If the way the Software handles information changes, this Privacy Policy will be updated and the effective date at the top of this page will change.
