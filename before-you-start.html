Everything the apps need, step by step.

The suite's apps work alongside a few free programs and accounts made by other people. Part 1 gets them ready before you install anything. Part 2 sets up each app once it's installed. You only need the steps for the apps you plan to use: the table shows which.

Part 1

Before you install

Step 1 · every app

Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024

Needed by every appTime as long as the sim's own download
  1. Buy the sim from Steam or from the Microsoft Store (see flightsimulator.com for every way to buy it). Any edition works.
  2. Install it and start it once. The first start downloads a large amount of data; let it finish, and go through the sim's first-time setup until you reach the main menu.
  3. Close the sim before you install the suite's apps.
Flight Deck Manager finds both the Steam and the Microsoft Store versions by itself.
Step 2 · for Squawk

A VATSIM account

Needed by Squawk, and to fly on the VATSIM networkCost freeTime about an hour, with the orientation course

VATSIM is the free online network where pilots fly together with real people controlling the traffic. You need an account before vPilot can connect.

  1. Sign up at my.vatsim.net/register with your real name and email, and choose your region and division. You must be at least 16 years old to join.
  2. Confirm your email address. VATSIM then sends your VATSIM ID (a number) and password. If the email doesn't arrive, check your junk folder.
  3. Sign in at my.vatsim.net and complete the New Member Orientation: a short course and its exam. You can't connect to the network until you've passed it.
Only ever make one VATSIM account. If you had one before, reactivate it at my.vatsim.net instead of making a new one. VATSIM's own guide: Join VATSIM.
Step 3 · for Squawk

vPilot

Needed by SquawkCost freeTime about 10 minutes

vPilot is VATSIM's pilot client for Microsoft Flight Simulator: it connects the sim to the network and carries your radio. Squawk is a plugin that runs inside vPilot.

  1. Download vPilot from its official page, vpilot.rosscarlson.dev/Download.
  2. Open the downloaded file to start the installer. Keep the folder it suggests (it needs no administrator rights), and finish the installer.
  3. Start vPilot. The first time, it says it isn't set up yet: select Yes to open its Settings.
  4. On the Network tab, enter your VATSIM ID and password, your full name and your home airport, and pick the VATSIM server closest to you.
  5. On the Audio tab, choose your microphone and your headset or speakers, then set the microphone volume while speaking normally.
  6. On the PTT tab, select Set new PTT Key or Button and press the key or joystick button you'll hold to talk.
  7. Close vPilot. It finds Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 by itself; there's no simulator to choose.
Keep vPilot closed while Squawk is installed. Squawk goes into vPilot's plugins, and from then on it starts whenever vPilot does. vPilot's own guide: vPilot documentation.
Step 4 · for Squawk

Tailscale

Needed by Squawk; also Logbook's Share my flightCost free for personal useTime about 10 minutes

Tailscale makes a private, encrypted link between your PC and your phone, so Squawk on the phone can reach vPilot on the PC from anywhere, with nothing to change on your router. It goes on both the PC and the phone, signed in to the same account.

On the PC

  1. Download Tailscale for Windows from tailscale.com/download/windows and open the file to install it.
  2. Sign in. Select the Tailscale icon near the clock (it may be under the ^ arrow), choose Log in, and sign in in the browser with a Google, Microsoft, Apple or GitHub account. Use one you can also sign in with on your phone.

On the iPhone or iPad

  1. Install Tailscale from the App Store (it needs iOS 15 or later).
  2. Open it and accept its prompts to add a VPN configuration and allow notifications.
  3. Sign in with the same account as on the PC. Both the phone and the PC now appear in the app's list of devices.

Turn on secure links (once)

  1. Open the DNS page of Tailscale's admin console (signed in with the same account).
  2. Make sure MagicDNS is on. Then, under HTTPS Certificates, select Enable HTTPS and confirm.
Turning on HTTPS lists your devices' names and your network's name in a public record of certificates. Give your PC a name you're happy for others to see: Tailscale's admin console › Machines › the PC's … menu › Edit machine name. Tailscale's own guide: Enabling HTTPS.
That's all for now. Putting Squawk on your phone comes after it's installed: see Part 2.
Step 5 · for Ramp

FSLTL Base Models

Needed by RampCost freeTime depends on your connection: a large download

FSLTL (FS Live Traffic Liveries) is a free collection of airliner models in real airlines' colours. Ramp parks these aircraft at the gates. Ramp needs only the Base Models; FSLTL's Traffic Injector isn't needed.

  1. Close Microsoft Flight Simulator.
  2. Get the FlyByWire Installer, which installs FSLTL: on fslivetrafficliveries.com, select Download via the FlyByWire Installer, then download and install it from flybywiresim.com.
  3. Point it at MSFS 2024's Community folder. In the FlyByWire Installer, select the gear icon (bottom left), then Download, and set the folders to Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024's own folders, not those of an older sim. Unless you chose another place when you installed the sim, the Community folder is:
    Steam: %APPDATA%\Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024\Packages\Community
    Microsoft Store: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages\Microsoft.Limitless_8wekyb3d8bbwe\LocalCache\Packages\Community
  4. Install the Base Models. Select FSLTL in the installer's list, choose FSLTL Base Models and select Install. Let it finish before closing the installer.
If you chose your own folder when you installed the sim, the Community folder is inside the folder you chose. FSLTL's own guide: FSLTL installation.
Step 6 · usually nothing to do

Microsoft Edge WebView2

Needed by Ramp and the Manager's windowCost free

WebView2 is the Microsoft component that draws the windows of Ramp and Flight Deck Manager. It's part of Windows 11 and is already on most Windows 10 PCs. Flight Deck Manager checks for it and installs it from Microsoft if it's missing, so most people have nothing to do here.

To check or install it yourself

  1. Check: open Windows Settings › Apps › Installed apps and look for Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime. If it's there, you're done.
  2. Install: on Microsoft's WebView2 page, under Evergreen Bootstrapper, select Download, then open the downloaded file.
Step 7 · optional

OBS Studio

Needed by Playback's video clips only (replays and screenshots work without it)Cost freeTime about 5 minutes
  1. Download OBS Studio for Windows from obsproject.com/download and open the file to install it, keeping the choices it suggests.
  2. Start OBS. If its Auto-Configuration Wizard opens, you can cancel it: Playback records with its own OBS profile and leaves yours as it is.
  3. Switch on OBS's remote control: Tools › WebSocket Server Settings › tick Enable WebSocket server › OK. Leave the port and password as they are; Logbook reads them from OBS's settings.
  4. Have OBS open when you play back a landing you'd like to film. Clips are saved to Videos\Flight Deck Suite\Playback and appear on that flight's logbook page.
Playback adds two scenes named "Flight Replay" to your OBS scene collection; nothing else in OBS is changed.
Step 8 · optional, GSX users only

GSX's Remote Control Server

Used by Ramp, to keep the gate you pick in GSX clearCost included with GSX, a separate add-on

If you use GSX for ground services, Ramp can read the gate you choose in GSX and keep it, and the stands beside it, clear of parked aircraft.

  1. In GSX's settings, switch on Remote Control Server.
  2. That's all. Ramp's Settings then show that GSX is connected and which gate it has. Ramp only reads from GSX; it never sends GSX a command or changes a GSX setting.
Step 9 · recommended

A SimBrief account

Used by Logbook (plan against actual, arrival time zone), Squawk (your route on the map, arrival time, top of descent) and Ramp (your arrival airport)Cost freeTime about 5 minutes

SimBrief makes realistic flight plans. When the apps know your SimBrief username, they read your latest plan from SimBrief. Without it, everything else still works.

  1. Sign up at simbrief.com. SimBrief is part of Navigraph, so signing up makes a free Navigraph account; no subscription is needed.
  2. Find your username: open SimBrief's Account Settings and, under Your Navigraph Account, note the Alias (Username). That's the name the apps need (not your email address, and not your Pilot ID).
  3. Plan each flight in SimBrief as usual. The apps always read the latest plan you made.
Where to enter the username comes in Part 2.
Step 10 · optional

A GitHub account

Used by Share my flight's always-on page (works even with your PC off)Cost freeTime about 5 minutes

Share my flight gives friends and family a live page of your flight. With a free GitHub account, Logbook also keeps a copy of it online that stays up when your PC is off. Skip this step if you don't want the always-on page.

  1. Sign up at github.com/signup with your email address and a password.
  2. Choose your username with care: it becomes part of your page's address, for example your-name.github.io/flight.
  3. Verify your email address with the code GitHub sends you.
The rest (a page and a key for Logbook) comes in Part 2.

Part 2

After you install

Once Flight Deck Manager has installed your apps, set each one up here. Skip the apps you didn't install.

Part 2 · Logbook

Logbook's first start

Time about 2 minutes
  1. Open Logbook from Flight Deck Manager (Open) or the Start menu. Its logbook site opens in your browser.
  2. Add your SimBrief username: in Logbook's Settings, enter it under SimBrief username. Ramp reads it from there too.
  3. Fly. Logbook notices the sim by itself and logs each flight, with nothing to press. Playback and Hangar are pages of the same site.
To have Logbook open by itself whenever the sim starts, switch on Start with MSFS for Logbook in Flight Deck Manager (with the sim closed).
Part 2 · Squawk

Squawk on your iPhone or iPad

Needs Steps 2 to 4 done, with Tailscale signed in on the PC and the phoneTime about 5 minutes

Open Squawk on the phone

  1. Start vPilot on the PC. Squawk starts with it.
  2. Find your PC's name in Tailscale: open the Tailscale app on the phone; your PC is in the list of devices.
  3. Open Safari on the phone and go to http:// + your PC's name + :8765, for example http://my-sim-pc:8765. Squawk opens.

Make the secure link and put Squawk on the Home Screen

  1. In Squawk, open Settings › Notifications and select Set up secure link. This uses the HTTPS you turned on in Step 4. If Squawk asks for anything more, follow its message, then try again.
  2. Select the Open button with your secure address (it starts with https:// and ends with .ts.net).
  3. Put Squawk on the Home Screen: in Safari, select Share, then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. From now on, open Squawk from its Home Screen icon.

Turn on alerts and your plan

  1. Notifications: in Squawk (opened from the Home Screen), Settings › Notifications › Turn on notifications, then Allow. iPhone notifications need iOS 16.4 or later. Send a test checks they arrive.
  2. SimBrief: in Squawk's Settings, enter your SimBrief username so the map follows your filed route.
If you said no to notifications by mistake, turn them back on in the iPhone's Settings › Notifications, then reopen Squawk.
Part 2 · Logbook · optional

Share my flight: your live link

Needs Logbook, and Tailscale on the PC (Step 4)Time about 5 minutes

A public, read-only page of your flight for friends and family: where you are, your route and your last landing. Nobody can change anything on it.

  1. In Logbook, open Settings › Share page and switch on Share my flight.
  2. Logbook asks Tailscale to publish your link. If it shows Approve, select Open Tailscale, approve the public link for this PC (Tailscale calls this Funnel) and come back to select Try again.
  3. If it shows Admin, Windows needs administrator rights once: right-click the Start button, choose Terminal (Admin) (on Windows 10, Windows PowerShell (Admin)), paste the line Logbook shows, press Enter, then select Try again.
  4. When it shows Live, select Copy link and send it to whoever you like.
The live link works while your PC and Logbook are on. For a page that stays up with the PC off, add the always-on page below. You choose which parts of your career show on the page, under Show my career.
Part 2 · Logbook · optional

Share my flight: the always-on page

Needs the live link above, and a GitHub account (Step 10)Time about 10 minutes, once

Logbook uploads a copy of your share page to GitHub Pages, GitHub's free website hosting, so it stays up with your PC off. It takes three things on GitHub: a repository (a folder for the page), its Pages switched on, and a key that lets Logbook upload to that one repository only.

1 · Make the repository

  1. Sign in to GitHub and open github.com/new (or the + menu at the top right › New repository).
  2. Repository name: a short name, for example flight. It becomes the end of your page's address.
  3. Choose Public, and turn on the option to add a README.
  4. Select Create repository.

2 · Switch on its Pages

  1. In the new repository, open Settings › Pages (in the list on the left).
  2. Under Build and deployment: Source Deploy from a branch; Branch main and / (root); then Save.

3 · Make the key

  1. Open GitHub's new fine-grained token page (or your picture at the top right › Settings › Developer settings › Personal access tokens › Fine-grained tokens › Generate new token).
  2. Token name: for example Flight Deck Suite share page.
  3. Expiration: choose No expiration, or the longest time offered. When a key expires, uploads stop until you make a new one and paste it into Logbook.
  4. Repository access: choose Only select repositories, then pick the repository you just made.
  5. Permissions: under the repository permissions, set Contents to Read and write. Leave everything else as it is.
  6. Select Generate token, then copy the key straight away (it starts with github_pat_). GitHub shows it only once.

4 · Give it to Logbook

  1. In Logbook, Settings › Share page › Set up your always-on page › Set it up.
  2. Repository: your username and the repository's name, for example your-name/flight. Key: paste the key.
  3. Select Save. Logbook checks both with GitHub and tells you if anything is wrong. The key is kept on your PC, encrypted, and is never shown again.
  4. The first upload takes a few minutes. Your link then points to the always-on page, for example your-name.github.io/flight.
Treat the key like a password: never post it or send it to anyone. If it's ever shared by mistake, delete it on GitHub's token page and make a new one.
Part 2 · the rest

Ramp and Playback

  1. Ramp needs nothing more: with FSLTL installed (Step 5) it fills the empty stands by itself when you're at an airport. If you use GSX, Step 8 lets it keep your gate clear.
  2. Playback needs nothing more for replays and screenshots. For video clips, have OBS Studio open (Step 7) when you play a landing back.
